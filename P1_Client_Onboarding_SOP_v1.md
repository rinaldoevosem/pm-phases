**CLIENT ONBOARDING**

Standard Operating Procedure & Phase Gate Document

v1.0 \| February 2026 \| Confidential

**PURPOSE & SCOPE**

This SOP defines the standard process for transitioning a signed client
from the sales handoff into an active, work-ready project state at Fancy
Lab. It ensures all operational logistics are established —
communication cadence, brand asset collection, credential verification,
design-readiness inputs, and team alignment — so that no ambiguity,
missing information, or unmet expectation blocks the project once work
begins. This phase is the bridge between the commercial agreement (P0)
and the technical kickoff (P2).

**Phase Objective**

Establish the complete working relationship with the client — including
communication expectations, feedback processes, approval authority,
asset handoff, and technical access verification — while simultaneously
preparing the internal team with all context and design-specific inputs
needed to begin high-quality work immediately in P2.

**Scope Includes**

> \- Welcome meeting scheduling and execution within 1 week of deposit
>
> \- Onboarding presentation delivery (team intro, phase overview,
> client expectations)
>
> \- Communication cadence and SLA agreement (response times, meeting
> frequency, channels)
>
> \- Stakeholder mapping — who approves design, content, technical, and
> launch decisions
>
> \- Feedback process setup — how will the client provide feedback
> (Figma, email, Loom, etc.)
>
> \- Brand asset collection, verification, and organization (logos,
> fonts, colors, photography)
>
> \- Design-specific input gathering (competitor analysis, hero vision,
> CTA tone, motion preference)
>
> \- Content readiness assessment — page-by-page status of existing copy
>
> \- Technical access verification (Shopify admin, domain, analytics,
> payment, email marketing)
>
> \- Existing site audit data collection (for migrations — traffic,
> rankings, URL structure)
>
> \- Client expectations and working style documentation
>
> \- Internal team readiness preparation (Slack channel, Drive folder,
> ClickUp board, team assignment)
>
> \- Project Brief review and confirmation with client
>
> \- Onboarding Intake Form (Appendix A) completion

**Scope Excludes**

> \- Project scoping, pricing, or proposal changes (return to P0 for
> Change Order)
>
> \- Technical project setup — ClickUp workspace, Figma project, dev
> store (covered in P2)
>
> \- Design or development work of any kind
>
> \- Content creation, copywriting, or SEO strategy (covered in P3)
>
> \- Product data collection or inventory management (covered in P5)

**HOW - PROCESS STEPS**

> **1. Send Welcome Email with Preparation Checklist**
>
> Within 24 hours of deposit confirmation, send the client a structured
> welcome email including: introduction to their assigned PM and team,
> overview of what to expect in the onboarding meeting, a preparation
> checklist of items the client should have ready (brand assets,
> platform logins, content status, competitor URLs, key decision-maker
> availability), meeting scheduling link, and the communication
> expectations outline for them to review before the meeting.
>
> **2. Schedule and Prepare Onboarding Meeting**
>
> Coordinate the onboarding meeting within the first week. Before the
> meeting: customize the onboarding presentation deck with the client's
> name and project type, review the completed Project Brief from P0 to
> identify areas that need clarification or deeper input, prepare a list
> of design-specific questions based on the project type (e.g., for
> jewelry sites: diamond search UI preferences, product photography
> style; for wellness sites: mood/tone imagery, appointment booking
> flow).
>
> **3. Conduct Onboarding Meeting**
>
> Walk the client through the onboarding presentation covering: Fancy
> Lab team introduction (who they will work with and each person's
> role), project phases overview with visual timeline, communication
> cadence proposal (meeting frequency, response SLAs, channels), how to
> provide feedback (Figma annotations, email with screenshots, Loom
> recordings), approval process (who signs off on design, content,
> launch), client responsibilities and their impact on timeline, key
> milestone dates. Record the meeting and share the recording within 24
> hours.
>
> **4. Establish Communication Expectations**
>
> During or immediately after the meeting, formally agree on: primary
> day-to-day communication channel, urgent issue channel, client
> response SLA (default: 3 business days), regular check-in cadence and
> format, who consolidates feedback if multiple stakeholders, approval
> authority for each area (design, content, technical, launch). Document
> in the Onboarding Intake Form and send a confirmation email to the
> client summarizing the agreed communication framework.
>
> **5. Collect and Verify Brand Assets**
>
> Systematically collect all brand assets: logo files in all formats
> (SVG, PNG transparent, AI/EPS, dark/light versions, favicon variant),
> confirmed brand colors with hex codes, font files or verified
> Google/Adobe Fonts links with licenses, brand guidelines PDF (if
> available), existing marketing materials and packaging for design
> reference, photography library, video assets,
> patterns/textures/illustrations. Verify quality and completeness of
> each item. For assets that are missing or inadequate, create a
> specific action list with deadlines.
>
> **6. Gather Design-Specific Inputs**
>
> Using Section 4 of the Intake Form, collect design-critical
> information that goes beyond the Project Brief: competitor visual
> analysis (what to emulate vs. differentiate), homepage hero vision and
> above-the-fold priorities, navigation complexity preference, product
> page element priority ranking, trust/credibility signal inventory, CTA
> language and tone preference, animation/motion level preference,
> mobile-first considerations (mobile traffic percentage), accessibility
> requirements, and any special UI component requests. These inputs
> allow the design team to start P4 with clear client direction.
>
> **7. Assess Content Readiness**
>
> Page by page, assess the status of all content: Homepage copy
> (ready/draft/needs writing), About page, Collection/Category
> descriptions, Product descriptions (how many complete?), FAQ content,
> Testimonials/reviews, Team bios, Legal pages. For each incomplete
> item, document the expected completion date and who is responsible.
> Establish a firm content deadline that ensures design can start with
> real copy, not placeholders.
>
> **8. Verify Technical Access and Credentials**
>
> Systematically verify every technical access point: Shopify admin
> (collaborator access granted?), domain registrar (login verified, DNS
> access confirmed), Google Analytics (GA4 property ID, access granted),
> Google Search Console (property verified, access granted), Google Tag
> Manager (container ID, access granted), payment gateway (Stripe/PayPal
> connected, test mode available), email marketing platform (API key,
> list IDs), all third-party app accounts. For each: confirm access
> works, document credentials securely, note any pending items.
>
> **9. Audit Existing Site Data (Migrations Only)**
>
> For migration projects, collect baseline data: current monthly traffic
> and sources, mobile vs. desktop split, top 10 landing pages with
> pageviews, current conversion rate, top SEO keywords and positions,
> URL structure samples for redirect mapping, known broken links/404s,
> current PageSpeed scores, customer accounts to migrate (count), order
> history requirements, blog post migration scope, and any third-party
> data that needs preservation (reviews, loyalty points, email lists).
>
> **10. Document Client Expectations and Working Style**
>
> Capture softer but critical information: client web savviness level
> (impacts communication style), previous agency experience (good or bad
> — what to replicate or avoid), biggest project fear, measurable
> definition of success (90-day post-launch), post-launch training
> needs, maintenance retainer interest, and reference/case study
> willingness. This information helps the team tailor their approach and
> avoid relationship pitfalls.
>
> **11. Prepare Internal Team**
>
> Before closing P1: create the project Slack channel and add all team
> members, organize the Google Drive project folder with proper
> subfolders (Brand, Content, Credentials, Reference, Deliverables),
> prepare the ClickUp board with project template and initial
> assignments, brief the team via Slack with: project summary, client
> communication style notes, key deadlines, design direction summary,
> risk flags, and links to all project resources.
>
> **12. Review Project Brief with Client and Finalize**
>
> Walk through the P0 Project Brief with the client to confirm every
> assumption is still accurate. Cross-reference with information
> gathered during onboarding. If any discrepancies or scope changes are
> identified, document them. Minor clarifications are noted directly.
> Scope changes are routed back to P0 for a Change Order before
> proceeding.
>
> **13. Send Project Timeline Confirmation and Close Phase**
>
> Send a formal email to the client confirming: final project timeline
> with milestone dates, confirmed communication cadence, next steps (P2
> Kickoff), what the client should expect to happen next and by when,
> and the Fancy Lab team contact information. Internally, post the phase
> completion notification in Slack and mark P1 as complete in ClickUp.

**ESCALATION - BLOCKED PHASE PROTOCOL**

|  |  |  |  |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Trigger** | **Timeframe** | **Escalation Action** | **Escalated To** |

| Assets/credentials still missing after first follow-up | 5 business days | Second follow-up; copy PM on email; note potential timeline impact | Carlos + Rinaldo |
| Assets/credentials still missing after second follow-up | 10 business days | Phone call to client; formally notify of timeline delay; document impact | Rinaldo |
| Client requests scope changes during onboarding | Immediately | Document the change request; route to P0 for Change Order process; do not proceed with changed scope | Harry + Rinaldo |

**REVISION LIMITS & SCOPE CONTROL**

Onboarding Presentation: Standard template used; client-specific
customization is one-time per project

Timeline Adjustments: Timeline can be adjusted once during onboarding
based on client availability; further changes require PM approval and
client notification

Communication Cadence: Agreed upon once; changes require mutual
agreement documented in email

*Any additional revisions beyond the limits above require a Change Order
approved by the Project Lead and communicated to the client with
associated timeline and cost impact.*
